This research was conducted jointly with Julian-Ferdinand Vögele of Recorded Future’s Insikt Group. Details of the joint research and presentation are available here . Cisco Talos identified UAT-6275 ( TAG-193 by Recorded Future) activity targeting government organizations across the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS). Recovered artifacts show additional interest in water resources and energy. This focus is consistent with intelligence collection and may reflect the regional effects of the Russia–Ukraine war. In activity observed from June 2025 through August 2026, Talos identified four infection-chain types that use macro-enabled documents, PS2EXE downloaders, GitHub-hosted PowerShell, and a crafted WinRAR archive exploiting CVE-2025-8088. The recovered payloads include several custom reverse shells that provide interactive remote access. Three publicly accessible staging directories and GitHub repository history exposed payload builds, tool inventories, and changes to command-and-control (C2) infrastructure. These operational security lapses gave us direct visibility into the group's recent activity. The files recovered from the exposed infrastructure suggest preparation for post-compromise operations and potential activity against Linux hosts and internet-facing appliances. Talos assesses with medium confidence that UAT-6275 has a Kazakhstan nexus, based on infrastructure access, language artifacts, sample submissions, working hours analysis, and regional targeting interests. Background Cisco Talos and Recorded Future Insikt Group jointly investigated UAT-6275 activity targeting government organizations across the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS). The investigation uncovered three publicly accessible staging directories and a previously unreported GitHub repository used to host malicious scripts and payloads. These exposed assets provided visibility into the threat actor's malware development, infrastructure changes and preparations for additional intrusion activity. Talos tracks this activity as UAT-6275, while Recorded Future tracks it as TAG-193. It overlaps with Silent Lynx (first reported by Seqrite) and sits within a broader set of campaigns connected to YoroTrooper , which we first reported in 2023. Subsequent research on Tomiris (Kaspersky) , ShadowSilk (Group-IB) , Cavalry Werewolf (BI.ZONE) , and Hydra Saiga (VMRay) identified overlapping indicators across these reported clusters. We assess that UAT-6275 and YoroTrooper likely belong to the same broader activity cluster. However, we continue to track them separately because we have not observed direct indicator or toolset overlap between the two. This post summarizes our joint Virus Bulletin 2026 white paper, “ Break the silence: tracking Silent Lynx through exposed infrastructure ” which contains the full technical analysis and indicators of compromise (IOCs). Victimology UAT-6275's targeting is concentrated in Central Asia and the wider CIS region. Across the reviewed campaigns, observed victims and lure themes span nine countries: Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Russia, and Belarus. Figure 1. Regional targeting. Government and diplomatic affairs UAT-6275 targets government institutions responsible for diplomacy, economic development, agriculture, and environmental management. These organizations hold information that can inform bilateral negotiations, economic planning, and regional security decisions. Our network flow analysis identified activity involving a Tajik government entity shortly after treaty discussions with Kazakhstan in March 2026 and a Belarusian government entity ahead of a bilateral meeting in April. This timing may indicate an interest in regional diplomatic developments. The decoy documents we recovered also suggest an interest in diplomatic relations, international trade, and aviation. Examples include a diplomatic letter addressed to Armenia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Figure 2. Lure document masquerading as a Russian diplomatic congratulatory letter to the Armenian Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Water security and resource management The group’s interest in regional resources spans environmental management and energy. Talos observed activity involving an Armenian government entity responsible for environmental matters and Uzbekistan’s Smart Forestry initiative, consistent with earlier reporting on Silent Lynx targeting water resource organizations across Central Asia. Energy-themed decoys included a cooperation proposal between Turkmenistan’s state oil company, Turkmennebit, and Russia’s Orsk Oil Refinery, used in an August 2025 sample. The refinery supplies petroleum products to Kazakhstan and later shut down after a Ukrainian drone strike in August 2026 , with repairs expected to take up to six months. These ties suggest a possible intelligence interest in regional fuel supplies and the war’s implications for Kazakhstan’s energy security. Figure 3. Decoy impersonating Turkmennebit and proposing cooperation with Orsk Oil Refinery management. Campaign timeline Since Seqrite first reported Silent Lynx in January 2025, we have identified at least eight campaigns across the region through May 2026. These include malware delivery targeting Russia, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan; a credential-harvesting site impersonating Azerbaijan’s State Agency for Public Service; and lures referencing the China–Central Asia Summit and Russian energy infrastructure. In 2026, we also observed activity involving government entities in Tajikistan, Armenia, and Belarus, as well as Uzbekistan’s Smart Forestry initiative in May. Figure 4. Campaign timeline. Infrastructure and malware delivery Talos identified three publicly accessible staging directories containing malware and tools of UAT-6275. One sample led to the previously unreported GitHub repository, where the group hosted PowerShell scripts and payloads. Its commit history allowed us to track infrastructure changes and malware development. The group relies on lightweight reverse shells, including Chvost, Laplas, and PickleShell, to issue commands on compromised systems. We also recovered Linux-compatible tooling and a publicly available proof-of-concept exploit for FortiWeb CVE-2025-25257 from UAT-6275 infrastructure. These findings suggest interest in expanding the group’s operational capabilities. Four infection chains UAT-6275 used malicious documents, executable downloaders, and shortcuts to deliver malware, and later incorporated a WinRAR exploit. Talos categorized the recovered samples into four infection chains, A – D, based on how they retrieve and execute their payloads. Figure 5. First observations of four UAT-6275 infection chains, June 2025 – April 2026. The first Chain A samples, observed in June 2025, used macro-enabled Word documents to download and execute a second-stage binary. In July 2025, we observed Chain B downloaders built with PS2EXE, a tool that packages PowerShell scripts as standalone Windows executables. These downloaders retrieved reverse-shell payloads and created scheduled tasks for persistence. We first observed Chain C in October 2025. A malicious shortcut launches PowerShell to retrieve and execute a GitHub-hosted script that provides remote shell access. Later samples also downloaded PickleShell and placed it in the Windows Startup folder for persistence. Figure 6. Chain C retrieves a GitHub-hosted PowerShell script; later samples also deploy PickleShell to the Startup folder. In April 2026, we identified a RAR archive that exploited CVE-2025-8088 in WinRAR to place PickleShell in the Windows Startup folder (Chain D). The payload runs at the next user sign-in. This was the first vulnerability exploit we observed in UAT-6275’s malware delivery. Figure 7. Chain D exploits CVE-2025-8088 to place PickleShell in the Windows Startup folder for execution at the next sign-in. Kazakhstan nexus assessment Talos assesses with medium confidence that UAT-6275 has a Kazakhstan nexus. Supporting evidence includes Russian-language artifacts, apparent malware-testing submissions from Kazakhstan shortly after compilation, and operator activity consistent with UTC+5 working hours. Figure 8. Timing of 77 GitHub commits in UTC+3 and UTC+5, with 09:00–18:00 local working hours shaded. Looking ahead UAT-6275’s sustained activity and adoption of new delivery techniques suggest the group will continue pursuing access to government organizations across Central Asia and the CIS. Its interest in environmental agencies, economic ministries, and energy infrastructure reflects the intelligence value of regional diplomacy and resource management. Defenders in these sectors should monitor unexpected PowerShell activity and payload downloads from public services such as GitHub, alongside scheduled tasks and Startup-folder changes. Keeping archive utilities patched can help reduce exposure to the exploit-assisted delivery observed in this campaign. For further details on this research and our accompanying presentation, see “ Break the Silence: Tracking Silent Lynx Through Exposed Infrastructure ” on Virus Bulletin’s website, as well as Recorded Future’s related blog post . In this article Uncovering the shadow world of a phishing kit ecosystem Storm-3119: YAM Store, the power broker Storm-2990: The Iron Man System phishing kit Downstream activity from Storm-2990 phishing kits Mitigation and protection guidance Microsoft Defender detections Over the past year, Microsoft uncovered a Chinese-language cybercriminal ecosystem, an interconnected network of phishing kit operators, developers, and an illicit marketplace selling tools and infrastructure for large-scale phishing, enabling cybercriminal operations worldwide. Threat actors purchased and used these tools and infrastructure, including phishing kits and access to compromised or fraudulent Microsoft or Google cloud environments and related credentials, to target organizations across the healthcare, financial services, wholesale distribution, and information technology (IT) sectors in Japan, the United States, Australia, and India. In collaboration with law enforcement agencies and other partners worldwide, Microsoft’s Digital Crimes Unit (DCU) facilitated a disruption of the ecosystem’s primary developers and infrastructure . Microsoft’s research into one phishing kit unraveled a larger ecosystem, including the phishing kit developers, an infrastructure dealer, and the threat actors who buy from both. In this blog, we examine several phishing kits marketed and sold through YAM Store, an underground marketplace tracked by Microsoft as Storm-3119. We share our analysis of the technical aspects of this Chinese-language phishing kit ecosystem, the shared infrastructure, provisioning methods, and downstream activity by threat actors using these tools. Figure 1: Heat map of most impacted countries by combined Storm-3119 and Storm-2990-related activity. Uncovering the shadow world of a phishing kit ecosystem Microsoft, alongside industry partner Resecurity , identified three phishing kits sold through the YAM Store that make up this Chinese-language cybercriminal network: CoGUI (also known as NX or FA), Haozi (also known as Phoenix), and Iron Man System. Microsoft tracks the cluster of activity and the developers behind all three phishing kits as Storm-2990. Since January 2026, the threat actors behind these kits have been responsible for facilitating the sending of over one billion phishing emails and SMS messages to over 75 thousand organizations worldwide. Microsoft assesses with high confidence that these phishing kits were developed by Chinese-speaking threat actors, and, despite their distinct branding and separate Telegram-based sales channels, exhibit highly similar capabilities and functionality. The phishing kits provide phishing templates impersonating legitimate brands and organizations (including government, financial, and logistics) worldwide, and enable criminal actors to facilitate high-volume phishing and credential stealing operations. The similarity in their infrastructure and overlap in activity suggest either shared development origins, code reuse, or close collaboration among their developers. These phishing kits have been advertised through YAM Store, first CoGUI and Haozi, followed by Iron Man System starting in June 2026. YAM Store and affiliated phishing kit companies accepted payments through cryptocurrency, including USDT and Ethereum, adding another layer to conceal illicit activity. The scale and proliferation of this ecosystem demonstrate how readily available criminal infrastructure can lower the barrier to entry, enabling less skilled cybercriminals to target organizations at scale and contribute to fraud losses. By mapping the relationship between YAM Store and these phishing kits, we provide insight into the commercial infrastructure that supports modern phishing operations. Figure 2. YAM Store (Storm-3119) sells fraudulently acquired infrastructure to CoGUI, Haozi, and Iron Man System, all interconnected phishing kit operators, who then provide these services to other threat actors. Storm-3119: YAM Store, the power broker YAM Store is an initial access broker (IAB) that sells infrastructure using compromised or fraudulently obtained environments in providers like Microsoft and Google. The marketplace offers a variety of infrastructure based on age, subscription type, and environment types such as education (.edu) or non-profit (.org). Figure 3: YAM Store procurement and downstream activity chain The YAM Store marketplace provides cybercriminals with a platform to purchase fraudulently acquired Microsoft and Google domains associated with legitimate, expired, or previously used organization numbers, allowing actors to use seemingly legitimate infrastructure and obtain workspaces at relatively low cost. Storm-3119 uses automation to identify expired environment or organization names and leverages legitimate customer information associated with them to help circumvent existing detections. Figure 4. YAM Store interface and Telegram contact Anatomy of YAM Store operations: How Storm-3119 acquires, controls, and monetizes infrastructure During this investigation, Microsoft uncovered a five-stage attack pipeline often used in Storm-3119’s operations. YAM Store functions as an access broker, an intermediary that hijacks Microsoft 365 and Azure for Education tenants and resells the stolen accounts, licenses, and storage as a service to downstream operations, including the phishing kits tracked as Storm-2990. The pipeline below describes the supply side of that business: how the actor acquires, controls, and monetizes compromised environments. Stage 1 – Reconnaissance : Storm-3119 conducts automated reconnaissance of target environments. The actor uses Flask, a lightweight Python web framework, to covertly identify and profile cloud-enabled organizations. The tooling is designed to discover Microsoft 365 tenants, assess enrollment opportunities, gather licensing and Azure cloud information, and perform these activities anonymously through Tor infrastructure. Stage 2 – Environment takeover and app-only admin panels : Once a viable environment is identified, the threat actor centralizes associated information in an actor-controlled platform used to administer compromised Microsoft 365 environments. This platform is a separate set of administrative panels (implemented in PHP, Java/Spring, and Cloudflare Workers) that lets the operator maintain, monetize, and expand access using stolen application credentials. Leveraging the compromised environment’s client secret through an application-only authentication flow, the panel performs full directory control: it resets passwords silently (without forcing a change at next sign-in), assigns education licenses, and grants privileged attributes that the actor can later monetize. Because this access is application-level rather than tied to a single user session, the resulting persistence is more durable, more scalable, and harder to detect. Stage 3 – Storage and resource abuse : With the stolen privileged credentials, the threat actor signs in to a compromised Global Administrator account and self-provisions the PnP Management Shell service principal, which represents the actor’s application inside the compromised tenant. Through this service principal, the threat actor grants themselves broad Microsoft Graph permissions, including read and write access to directory data and AppCatalog.ReadWrite.All, along with full control over the SharePoint API. The actor then abuses the compromised tenant’s SharePoint and OneDrive as bulk hosting and converts the hijacked storage into a sellable product. Stage 4 – Phishing and DNS infrastructure support : The threat actor maintains dedicated infrastructure to support and manage phishing operations on behalf of their customers. A custom DNS management platform enables centralized control of phishing domains, SSL certificates, and domain-routing configurations through Cloudflare. Stage 5 – Storefront and monetization : Storm-3119 resells the hijacked accounts, licenses, and storage through an automated storefront, operating as the access broker that supplies these resources to downstream operations. Payment is accepted through Alipay, WeChat Pay, and USDT (TRC20), and the storefront supports a sub-reseller tier. Telegram is used for order notifications and buyer contact rather than as the primary sales channel. In addition to targeting existing tenants, Storm-3119 also separately creates fraudulent organizations and supporting web presence to establish tenants on Microsoft’s platform, likely using AI-generated content and forged documentation to make the fabricated entities appear legitimate. In one case, the actor created an environment, likely using AI, named “Jewish Choice” under the guise of a nonprofit organization and associated it with jewishchoice[.]org , a domain registered only within the past several months that did not appear to represent a legitimate organization. This activity indicates that Storm-3119 likely established the website and associated domain to lend credibility to the fabricated nonprofit identity and facilitate tenant creation. Figure 5: YAM Store created the fake non-profit “Jewish Choice” to sell to criminal actors with a [.]org top-level domain to appear as a legitimate organization in phishing emails. Storm-2990: The Iron Man System phishing kit YAM Store sells its illicitly acquired infrastructure, as well as the prominent phishing kits tracked as Storm-2990, to other threat actors. The platforms are commercially licensed phishing-as-a-service (PaaS) products. They are designed to impersonate organizations across several industry verticals to steal information, including sign-in credentials (usernames, passwords) and financial data (credit card information), used to gain access to network environments. Iron Man System’s customers purchase a subscription that provides access to the kit’s license server, with the domain hard-coded into the kit. This restriction adds a layer of obfuscation by limiting site access to paying customers. The kits allow attackers to steal money by abusing the stolen credit card information. Figure 6. Iron Man System admin sign-in The Iron Man System phishing kit contains over 2,335 templates tailored to over 139 countries including Australia, Japan, India, and the United States, with the United States accounting for the most phishing templates. The phishing lures used by Iron Man System mimic various trusted brands and organizations, such as local government including authorities associated with traffic and parking fees, law enforcement, healthcare, finance, transportation, and retail. These lures are designed to trick targets into providing sensitive information. Most phishing lures used in the United States are transportation or government-related. Figure 7. Phishing lure impersonating traffic authorities in the Philippines Figure 8. Phishing lure impersonating Amazon shipping services Figure 9. Phishing lure impersonating a Japanese financial service provider. It warns the target that a routine security check determined that the target’s account requires re-authentication. The email provides instructions for the target to visit the ‘official’ website and follow instructions to authenticate to the portal Figure 10. Sample lure impersonating Australia Post Figure 11. Sample lure impersonating Medicare The phishing kits are capable of the following advanced evasion techniques to varying degrees: Geofencing , a technique used to restrict access to configured countries only, so only users from specific countries can access the phishing content from their web browser Fake CAPTCHA gates , used to prevent or block automated browsing from threat research tools by making the user click a box or execute an action IP address block list and allow list , used to block or allow IP addresses to prevent threat researcher interaction Anti-detection configuration related tosettings to evade security researcher detection Telegram bot configuration ,including bot tokens and chat IDs for real-time phishing that enables credential exfiltration. It allows the kit operator to receive automatic notifications on their Telegram account when a target takes action on a phishing lure, including entering credentials, credit card data submission, and verification codes. After the target provides the sensitive information (sign-in details, credit card info), the phishing kit collects it using a WebSocket, offering customers real-time victim interaction through Telegram notifications during credential harvesting. This confirms that the kit supports live, interactive phishing where the operator monitors each victim’s progress and manually controls what the victim sees next, a hallmark of sophisticated PaaS platforms. The attacker then harvests the information to use in follow-on fraudulent financial activities. The Iron Man System phishing kit panel is predominately hosted on China-based hosting providers, with a smaller number of panels found on hosting providers in other countries. The fully featured, multi-tenant phishing kit uses the GoFrame application development framework, an open-source Go framework with roots in the Chinese developer ecosystem. Downstream activity from Storm-2990 phishing kits Microsoft observed downstream activity by threat actors using infrastructure affiliated with the Storm-2990 phishing kits. In analyzed cases, the actors used similar common phishing techniques, such as urgency in the message and attaching malicious links that redirect to actor-controlled infrastructure that allows these downstream threat actors to perform the following activities: Credential stealing: The actors use phishing pages that impersonate legitimate services to trick targets into providing credentials or other authentication information, enabling downstream actors to access compromised accounts. Device code phishing: In some cases, the actors abuse the device code authentication flow to trick victims into authenticating to legitimate applications, allowing downstream actors to obtain authentication tokens and access compromised accounts. Financial fraud: The end goal for the downstream actors is to use stolen payment data to make fraudulent purchases of high-end goods or fraudulent transactions on legitimate financial platforms. The threat actors using the phishing kits conduct phishing campaigns at scale whether it is a missing toll payment or a traffic citation. Mitigation and protection guidance This interconnected network of Chinese-language phishing kit operators and developers operates as an enabling ecosystem, providing infrastructure and services that facilitate criminal activity rather than functioning as a standalone malware tool. Microsoft assesses that the ecosystem is not limited to the abuse of Microsoft services and extends to other providers. While Microsoft notes that the identified organizations impacted by attacks stemming from Storm-3119 or Storm-2990 operations include financial, healthcare, and IT companies, the activity conducted by threat actors using these tools are common attacks that could affect any organization. Most of the activity that stems from this phishing kit ecosystem consists of social engineering, phishing operations, and account and storage takeovers. Microsoft recommends the following to mitigate the impact of Storm-3119 and Storm-2990-related threats. In nearly all the cases observed by Microsoft, the phishing lures used by the Storm-2990 operators followed prescriptive scam red flags, including urgency, time-sensitive tasks such as clicking a provided link with official branding. Users should verify with the purported sender of the message or email, even when the task involves signing in to a known, secure account not provided by the link or calling the purported organization or company directly. Educate users about protecting personal and business information in social media, filtering unsolicited communication, identifying lures in spear-phishing emails and watering holes, and reporting reconnaissance attempts and other suspicious activities. Educate users about common phishing techniques. Sign-in prompts should clearly identify the application being authenticated to. As of 2021, Microsoft Azure interactions prompt the user to confirm (Cancel or Continue) that they are signing in to the app they expect, which is an option frequently missing from phishing sign-ins. Review our recommended settings for Exchange Online Protection and Microsoft Defender for Office 365 to ensure your organization has established essential defenses and knows how to monitor and respond to threat activity. Configure the Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Safe Links policy to apply to internal recipients. Hardening credentials and cloud identities is also necessary to defend against phishing attacks, which seek to gain valid credentials and access tokens. As an initial step, use passwordless solutions like passkeys and implement multifactor authentication (MFA) throughout your environment: Invoke the principles of least privilege and Zero Trust to significantly slow down threat actors who might have been able to gain initial access and buy time for defenders to respond. Since Storm-3119 operators have been observed obtaining Global Administrator access, implementing these principles can help lock down Global Administrator roles and who has access to specific resources. Use the Microsoft Authenticator app for passkeys and MFA , and complement MFA with Conditional Access policies, where sign-in requests are evaluated using additional identity-driven signals. Conditional Access policies can also be scoped to strengthen privileged accounts with phishing-resistant MFA . Your passkey and MFA policy can be further secured by only allowing MFA and passkey registrations from trusted locations and devices. Enable network protection to prevent applications or users from accessing malicious domains and other malicious content on the internet.
