Quarterly figures In Q3 2026: Kaspersky products blocked more than 311 million attacks launched from online resources. Web Anti-Virus responded to 47 million unique links. File Anti-Virus blocked more than 23 million malicious and potentially unwanted objects. There were 4470 new ransomware variants discovered. More than 81,000 users experienced ransomware attacks. 15% of all ransomware victims whose data was published on threat actors’ data leak sites (DLSs) were victims of Qilin. More than 217,000 users were targeted by miners. Ransomware Quarterly trends and highlights Threat actor disruption In August, the creator and lead administrator of the Ransom Cartel ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) operation was sentenced to 16 years in prison. The convict developed the ransomware Trojan and at the time of his arrest played a central role in the RaaS operation: running the affiliate program, recruiting affiliates, supplying them with information and attack tools, working with initial access brokers, communicating with victims, and processing payments. In September, a member of the Ryuk RaaS group was sentenced to two years in prison and three years of supervised release. In 2019 and 2020, he and his accomplices breached organizations’ networks and encrypted data with the Ryuk Trojan. According to public reports, hundreds of workstations and servers were compromised. According to the prosecution, the attacker received a total of about 1610 BTC in ransom payments from victims. Also in September, a member of the Conti RaaS group was sentenced to four years in prison. He was charged with attacking companies in 2021 and 2022. In addition to carrying out attacks, he developed part of the group’s malware toolkit. Vulnerabilities and attacks In July, researchers published a report on the Qilin group exploiting the CVE-2026-0257 vulnerability in Palo Alto Networks firewall software. The vulnerability allowed attackers to bypass authentication and compromise victims’ networks without valid accounts. CISA published information in its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog (KEV) on several CVEs actively exploited by ransomware groups: CVE-2026-15409 / CVE-2026-15410 in SonicWall SMA1000 remote access gateways. CISA doesn’t specify which groups exploited these vulnerabilities, but researchers have linked this activity to INC Ransom. CVE-2026-45659 in Microsoft SharePoint Server allowed an authenticated, low-privileged attacker to achieve remote code execution. CVE-2025-60710 in Microsoft Windows allowed attackers to escalate privileges locally. CVE-2026-59310 in VMware vCenter enabled unauthenticated remote code execution. CVE-2025-14733 in WatchGuard Firebox also enabled unauthenticated remote code execution. CVE-2026-63077 in JetBrains TeamCity allowed OS commands to run with the privileges of the TeamCity server process by way of an authentication bypass through the agent polling protocol. Command execution at that level could give access to configurations, credentials, CI/CD pipelines, and more. Attacks on macOS Quarterly highlights At the beginning of the third quarter, Jamf researchers discovered a new stealer, PamStealer. What sets it apart is its use of Pluggable Authentication Modules (PAM) to verify the administrator password the user enters. During the quarter, Socket researchers discovered several supply chain attacks that compromised popular npm, PyPI and Cargo (the Rust package manager) packages. The malicious payloads in these packages targeted macOS systems along with others. For example, on July 11, an infected npm package, jscrambler, was published . Its malicious module was an infostealer written in Rust. The infostealer targeted crypto wallets, secrets from various services and browsers, MCP server configurations, and API keys for AI agents. In August, an attack was detected on the arrayref, internment, and append-only-vec Rust crates; it delivered a Rust backdoor to the victim’s device. In September, malicious versions of the MemTensor npm and PyPI packages were published . They delivered a Go backdoor that targeted secrets, configurations, and API tokens of various services. In August, Jamf researchers also reported discovering a new backdoor, Overlord . It was distributed as fake Zoom installers and had fairly broad spyware functionality, which is not often seen in macOS backdoors: it could eavesdrop through the microphone, watch the user through the camera, capture the screen, and log keystrokes. In the third quarter, we discovered an updated version of the MacSync crypto stealer and backdoor with a significantly changed payload delivery method. Mobile statistics The mobile section of the quarterly cyberthreat report includes statistics on malware, adware, and potentially unwanted software for Android, as well as descriptions of the most notable threats for Android and iOS discovered during the reporting period. These statistics are based on detection alerts from Kaspersky products, collected from users who consented to provide statistical data to Kaspersky Security Network. The quarter in figures According to Kaspersky Security Network, in Q3 2026: More than 1.66 million attacks involving mobile malware, adware, or unwanted software were blocked. The Trojan-Banker category was the prevalent mobile malware threat with a 31.63% share of all detected malicious apps. Over 378,000 malicious installation packages were detected, including 119,688 mobile banking Trojan packages. Quarterly highlights In July, we discovered a malicious app targeting Brazilian users that may have used AI to automate fraud. The app was disguised as Google Play Protect, a suite of security services for Android devices. Malicious app imitating Google Play Protect The app’s code used the DistilBERT model to interpret natural-language commands sent from the C2 server. Traces left in the code suggest the server-side commands may also have been generated by a large language model (LLM). In August, we discovered a Trojan that analyzed images saved in the device’s gallery and took screenshots to look for banking data, including CVV codes. The malware used an on-device AI model to classify images. The app could also update this model, which gave the scammers a way to expand the Trojan’s functionality. In the third quarter, the infamous subscription Trojan detected as Trojan.AndroidOS.Subscriber.bh made it into Google Play again. This time, it was disguised as an app for creating keyboard themes. Subscription Trojan disguised as a keyboard theme app The Trojan opened a subscription link in a hidden WebView and intercepted the message containing the confirmation code. According to our data, the scammers mainly targeted users in Thailand. There were also new attacks on iOS users. In August, researchers at Socket discovered 13 infected PHP packages in the Packagist repository. The malicious payload injected links to fraudulent casinos into sites that used these packages and, on iOS devices only, a hidden iframe that loaded the DarkSword exploit kit. The attack delivered a spyware module that stole keychain contents, Wi-Fi passwords, SMS messages, photos, and other personal data. # HONG BAO GROUP — DATA BREACH **Hong Bao Group** (หงเปา กรุ๊ป) — a major Thai restaurant conglomerate operating 15+ restaurant brands across Bangkok, including **Hong Bao Restaurant**, **Water Library**, **Lovely Wine**, **PLU (พลู)**, **CIAO PIZZA**, **Bistro**, and **SEED** — has suffered a complete network compromise resulting in the exfiltration of their entire corporate infrastructure. ## Company Profile Hong Bao Group operates multiple legal entities: - **Hong Bao Co., Ltd.** (บริษัท หงเปา จำกัด) - **Hong Bao EV Co., Ltd.** (บริษัท หงเปา อีวี จำกัด) — Tax ID: 0105559005630 - **Water & Wine (Thailand) Co., Ltd.** (บริษัท วอเตอร์ แอนด์ ไวน์ (ประเทศไทย) จำกัด) - **Lovely Wine Co., Ltd.** (บริษัท เลิฟลี่ไวน์ จำกัด) - **I-Group** (investment holding) The group operates restaurants in premium Bangkok locations including MBK Center, King Square, Ramada, and multiple other shopping malls across Thailand. **Websites:** hongbaorestaurant.com | waterlibrary.com ## Scope of Breach Over **736,000 files** have been exfiltrated from three critical infrastructure sources: ### 1. Corporate File Server (FL6-SRV-FILSHA) - **86,500+ files** from all corporate departments - Accounting (Hongbao + Water divisions) - Executive Administration — including **board meeting audio recordings** - Finance department — complete reports - HR — employee records, contracts, personal documents - Purchasing — vendor agreements, pricing ### 2. Company NAS (NAS5EECFF) - **647,000+ files** — the complete corporate document archive - **hongbao/** — 400,000+ files: finance, tax, HR, SAP ERP documentation - **water/** — 180,000+ files: Water & Wine financial, banking, payroll data - **hb_wl/** — shared financial and tax records - **Scanned documents** — thousands of signed originals ### 3. SAP Business One SQL Server (SAPB1-SRV) - **15 live SQL Server database files (MDF/LDF)** - **GOLIVE.mdf** (~62 GB) — the entire SAP B1 production database containing ALL business transactions, customers, vendors, pricing, and inventory since inception - **GOLIVE_EV.mdf** (~56 GB) — complete audit trail - **SBO-COMMON.mdf** (~10 GB) — user accounts and system configuration - **master.mdf** — SQL Server authentication database with login credentials - **1,918 branch database backup archives** covering 15 branches ## Data Categories Exfiltrated ### Financial Records (15,500+ files) Complete profit & loss statements, balance sheets, cash flow reports, and audit records across all branches spanning **2022–2026**. Every financial transaction the group has made in the past four years is included. ### Banking Information (28,400+ files) - **Bangkok Bank (BBL)** Corporate iCash — account statements, transfers - **Siam Commercial Bank (SCB)** Business Net — corporate banking records - **Kasikorn Bank (K-Cash Connect Plus)** — payment records - Personal **credit card statements** of company directors - Wire transfer confirmations, payment vouchers ### Tax & Revenue Records (5,100+ files) - VAT filings for all entities - Withholding tax certificates - Revenue Department submissions - Annual tax returns from 2022–2026 ### Payroll & HR (7,800+ files) - Complete **salary records** for all employees across all branches - Overtime calculations, bonus payments - Social Security (ประกันสังคม) contributions - Provident fund records - Employment contracts ### Email Archives (14 Outlook mailboxes) Complete email archives (.PST) of key personnel from both hongbaorestaurant.com and waterlibrary.com domains, including all attachments and internal communications. ### Personal Identity Documents - **Passport scans** and **Thai national ID cards** of company directors and executives - Visa and travel documents - Flight bookings and itineraries ### Contracts & Legal (2,800+ files) - Mall lease agreements for all restaurant locations - Vendor supply contracts - Non-disclosure agreements - Employment contracts with personal terms ### Wine & Alcohol Import Licenses (425+ files) All regulatory permits, excise department records, and import documentation for the Lovely Wine business — **without which the wine import business cannot legally operate**. ### POS & Customer Data - 15+ branch point-of-sale databases with transaction history - Customer checkout records - Daily sales reports across all locations ### IT Infrastructure Access - SAP B1 user accounts (303+ users) with password hashes - SQL Server administrator credentials - QNAP NAS access credentials - TSplus remote access configuration files - Internal network mapping (172.16.x.x) - Evidence of prior security compromise (CLR assemblies loaded in SQL Server) ## Key Personnel Exposed Personal documents, passport scans, banking records, and travel documents of the following individuals are included in the breach: - **Noppadon Narittakurn** — Director - **Tata** — Director - **Zithai** — Director / Designer - **Nitchaya Tohsanguanpun** — Director ## Impact This breach represents a **complete corporate data compromise**. Every department, every financial record, every employee record, every branch database, and every executive communication has been exfiltrated. The data includes sensitive regulatory documents (tax filings, alcohol import licenses) that could trigger **government investigations** if disclosed publicly. The personal banking records and passport scans of company directors present significant **identity theft and fraud risks**. The SAP production database alone contains the company's **entire operational history** — customer lists, vendor pricing, proprietary recipes and inventory formulas, and every financial transaction processed through their ERP system. **Data volume:** 736,000+ files | 15 SQL databases | 1,918 database backups | 14 email archives **Hong Bao Group has limited time to resolve this situation before full publication.** Ransomware group claim; not independently verified.
